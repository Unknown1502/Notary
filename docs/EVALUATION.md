## Why this document exists

Most AI review demos assert that their classifier works. This one measures it, and publishes where it cannot be measured. A compliance gate whose accuracy is unknown is a compliance gate you cannot deploy.

## The safety invariant (exhaustive proof)

`decide()` has a finite input space — a handful of criteria, each in one of four outcomes, crossed with check kind, severity, confidence band, and remaining revision budget. So it is not sampled, it is **enumerated completely**.

| Invariant | Combinations checked | Result |
|---|---:|---|
| A blocking FAIL or UNCERTAIN can never yield VERIFIED | 536,424 | **HOLDS** |
| An exhausted revision budget can never yield VERIFIED | 7 | **HOLDS** |

> Across every reachable combination, there is no input on which Notary certifies an asset that a blocking criterion failed or could not resolve. This is a stronger statement than an accuracy score: it is a property of the decision function, not an estimate of its behaviour.

## Deterministic checks — measured accuracy

Corpus of **32 samples**, of which **21 (66%) sit within ±10% of a decision threshold**. That concentration is deliberate: a corpus of obvious cases reports ~100% and tells you nothing, because a threshold classifier can only be wrong near its threshold.

Scored with `fail` as the positive class, since a missed violation is the error with consequences. **Recall is the number that matters** — a false alarm costs one render, a miss ships a non-compliant asset.

These scores are high because these checks are arithmetic, not inference. That is the argument for computing what can be computed: the measured half of the rubric is not a classifier that might be wrong, it is a measurement that is reproducible by anyone holding the file.

## What is not evaluated

The perceptual criteria are **not scored**:

- `logo_presence`
- `logo_legibility`
- `visual_artifacts`
- `tone_alignment`
- `prohibited_imagery`

Scoring these honestly requires real generated video and independent human labels. Synthesising them would measure our own assumptions, not the Board. The confidence floor of 0.55 is a reasoned default, not an empirically tuned value.

**Consequence.** Notary makes no accuracy claim about perceptual judgement. That is precisely why perceptual uncertainty escalates to a human instead of resolving automatically.

### How to close this gap

1. Assemble 30–50 real generated takes spanning pass, fail, and genuinely ambiguous.
2. Have two reviewers label each perceptual criterion independently; record inter-rater agreement, which upper-bounds any achievable score.
3. Run the Board and compute precision and recall per criterion, plus the escalation rate.
4. Tune the 0.55 confidence floor against the escalation rate a real team would tolerate.
5. Publish the table, including the criteria it does badly on.

The harness for steps 3–5 already exists in `backend/notary/evaluation/`. What is missing is labelled data, and inventing it would measure our own assumptions rather than the Board.
